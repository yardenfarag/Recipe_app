You found the perfect pasta on TikTok. Two weeks later you want to cook it, and it is gone: buried in your likes, or the caption just says "recipe in bio."

Here are four ways to save a TikTok recipe, starting with the ones built into TikTok, and what each one is good for.

## 1. Add it to your TikTok Favorites

Tap the **bookmark** icon on the right side of the video. TikTok keeps it under the bookmark tab on your profile, and you can sort favorites into collections like "Dinner" or "Baking."

**Good for:** a quick save while you scroll.

**The catch:** you still have to watch the whole video again to cook from it, pausing at every step. And if the creator deletes the video, it is gone from your favorites too.

## 2. Copy the caption

Many creators write the ingredients in the caption or pin them in a comment. Press and hold the caption to copy it, then paste it into your notes app.

**Good for:** videos with a full written recipe.

**The catch:** a lot of TikTok recipes are never written down. The amounts are only said out loud or shown on screen for a second.

## 3. Screenshot the ingredients

Pause on the frame that shows the ingredients and take a screenshot.

**Good for:** recipes that flash a full ingredient list on screen.

**The catch:** screenshots end up mixed in with every other photo on your phone, and they rarely include the steps.

## 4. Turn the video into a written recipe with Pinch

Pinch reads a TikTok cooking video and writes the recipe out for you: ingredients with amounts, numbered steps, and a shopping list.

1. On TikTok, tap **Share**, then **Copy link**.
2. Open Pinch, go to **Snap**, and paste the link. On iPhone you can also share the video straight to Pinch from the share sheet.
3. Check the preview and tap **Save recipe**. It lands in your Library.

Pinch gets the whole recipe, even when the amounts are only said out loud, shown on screen for a second, or tucked away in the comments.

Once it is saved you can:

- **Cook along** with the original video next to the steps.
- **Scale** the servings, or switch between cups and grams.
- **Send the ingredients to your shopping list**, grouped by aisle.
- **Sort it into a collection** with recipes saved from Instagram, YouTube, and recipe websites.

A free Pinch account includes 3 recipe credits a month, and you can buy more credits that never expire.

> **Tip:** Keep your TikTok favorites for inspiration, and save the recipes you actually plan to cook to Pinch. That way your "to cook" list stays short and every recipe in it is ready to use.

## Which way should you use?

| Method | Keeps ingredient amounts | Keeps steps | Survives the video being deleted | Works with the recipe only said out loud |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| TikTok Favorites | No | No | No | No |
| Copy the caption | Only if written | Only if written | Yes | No |
| Screenshot | Sometimes | Rarely | Yes | No |
| Pinch | Yes | Yes | Yes | Yes |

## Common questions

### Can I save a TikTok recipe without an account?

You need a TikTok account to use Favorites. You can copy a video's link without one. In Pinch, you can try a few extractions as a guest, and a free account lets you save them to your library.

### What if the creator says "recipe in bio"?

That usually means the recipe is on their blog. Open the link in their bio, copy the recipe page's address, and paste it into Pinch. It reads recipe websites too.

### Does it work for Instagram and YouTube?

Yes. The same steps work for Instagram Reels and YouTube videos. See [how to organize your Instagram saved recipes](#organize-instagram-saved-recipes).
