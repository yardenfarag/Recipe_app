The hard part of meal prep isn't the cooking. It's the shopping list. Three recipes that each need an onion turn into three separate "1 onion" lines, you buy cumin you already have, and you walk the store twice because dairy was at the bottom of the list.

Here is a simple way to build a meal-prep shopping list that avoids all three.

## 1. Pick your recipes first

Decide what you are cooking before you write anything down. For a week of meal prep, three or four recipes is plenty:

- **One or two big batch mains** that reheat well (chili, curry, a tray bake)
- **One grain or base** (rice, roasted potatoes, pasta salad)
- **One fresh thing** that keeps a few days (a slaw, a grain salad, cut vegetables)

Pick recipes that **share ingredients**. If one recipe uses half a bunch of cilantro, choose another that uses the rest.

## 2. Combine the ingredients

Put every ingredient from every recipe on one list, then merge the duplicates:

- 1 onion + 2 onions + 1 onion → **4 onions**
- 200 g rice + 300 g rice → **500 g rice**

Watch the units. "1 cup of rice" and "200 g of rice" are the same ingredient, but you have to convert one before you can add them up.

## 3. Cross off what you already have

Check your pantry before you shop. Salt, oil, and most spices are probably already there. Crossing them off now saves you from buying a third jar of paprika.

## 4. Sort the list by aisle

Group the list the way the store is laid out: produce, meat and fish, dairy and eggs, bakery, pantry, frozen, spices. You walk each section once, and nothing gets forgotten at the far end of the store.

## 5. Shop, check off, prep

Check items off as they go in the cart. When you get home, prep in order of what keeps the shortest: herbs and greens first, then proteins, then grains.

## The shortcut: let Pinch build the list

Steps 2 to 4 are where most of the time goes, and they are exactly what Pinch does for you:

1. **Save your recipes to Pinch.** From TikTok, Instagram, YouTube, a recipe website, or a photo of a cookbook page.
2. **Open List, tap From recipes**, and pick the recipes you are cooking this week.
3. **Matching ingredients stack into one line.** Four onions, not four lines of "onion."
4. **Mark pantry staples with "I keep this"**, and Pinch leaves them off your next list.
5. **The list is grouped by aisle:** produce, dairy and eggs, meat, seafood, bakery, pantry, frozen, and spices.

Then check things off in the store. If something is still missing, add it by hand.

## A sample meal-prep week

| Recipe | Role | Shares ingredients with |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Black bean chili | Batch main | Onion, cumin, peppers with the fajita tray bake |
| Sheet-pan chicken fajitas | Batch main | Onion, peppers, lime with the chili and slaw |
| Cilantro-lime rice | Base | Cilantro and lime with the slaw |
| Crunchy cabbage slaw | Fresh side | Cilantro, lime |

Four recipes, one short list, and nearly every fresh ingredient gets used up.

## Common questions

### How many recipes should I meal prep in a week?

Three or four. More than that and the list gets long, the fridge fills up, and food goes to waste before you get to it.

### How do I keep the list from growing every week?

Repeat what works. Keep a "Meal prep" collection of recipes that reheated well, and build next week's list from it.
