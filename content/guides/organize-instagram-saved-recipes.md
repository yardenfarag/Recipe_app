Saving a recipe on Instagram takes one tap. Finding it again takes a lot longer. After a few months, "Saved" is a wall of hundreds of thumbnails, and the recipe you want is somewhere in the middle.

Here is how to get it under control, first inside Instagram, then by moving the recipes you actually cook somewhere better.

## Step 1: Sort your saves into Instagram collections

Instagram lets you group saved posts into collections.

1. Go to your profile, open the menu, and tap **Saved**.
2. Create a few collections. Keep the list short. Five works better than twenty:
   - **Weeknight dinners**
   - **Baking**
   - **Meal prep**
   - **Hosting**
   - **Someday** (for the ambitious ones)
3. From now on, when you save a post, add it to a collection right away. On older saves, open the post and change its collection from the bookmark.

**The catch:** collections only fix the *finding*. You still have to watch the Reel to cook from it, and the amounts often aren't written anywhere.

## Step 2: Clear out what you will never cook

Be honest. Scroll through "All posts" and unsave anything you wouldn't make this month or next. A smaller list is one you actually use.

## Step 3: Move the recipes you cook into a recipe app

Instagram is built for discovering recipes, not cooking from them. When a recipe makes the cut, turn it into a written recipe with Pinch:

1. On the Reel, tap **Share**, then **Copy link**.
2. Open Pinch, go to **Snap**, and paste the link. On iPhone you can also share the Reel straight to Pinch.
3. Check the preview and tap **Save recipe**.

You get ingredients with amounts and numbered steps, even when the creator only said them out loud or showed them on screen.

## Step 4: Organize them in a way Instagram can't

Once your recipes are in Pinch, you have more ways to sort them than Instagram gives you:

- **Collections** that hold recipes from Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, recipe websites, and photos of cookbook pages, side by side.
- **Search** by recipe name or ingredient.
- **Tags** like "vegetarian" or "freezer-friendly."
- **Cook tonight**, a filter for recipes under 45 minutes and medium difficulty or easier.
- **What's in the fridge?** Snap a photo of your fridge, and Pinch ranks the recipes you already saved by what you have.

And because the recipe is written out, it doesn't disappear if the creator deletes the post.

## A system that stays organized

1. **Save freely on Instagram.** It is your inspiration board.
2. **Once a week, move the ones you'll cook to Pinch.** Put each one in a collection as you save it.
3. **Unsave the rest.**

It takes five minutes, and your recipe list stays small enough to use.

## Common questions

### Can I export all my Instagram saved posts at once?

Instagram's data download includes links to your saved posts, but not the recipes in them. Moving recipes one at a time is slower, but it is also what makes you clear out the ones you won't cook.

### Does this work for Instagram carousel posts?

If the recipe is written in the caption, yes. For a recipe written on a carousel image, screenshot that slide and use **Snap from a photo** in Pinch.

### What about TikTok?

The same idea works there. See [how to save a recipe from TikTok](#save-tiktok-recipes).
